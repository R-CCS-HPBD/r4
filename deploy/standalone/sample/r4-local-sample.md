# The Kestrel Bay tide gauge

This note is the sample document `./r4-local up` ingests to prove that the
local stack can store a document and answer a question from it. It was
written for this repository and is dedicated to the public domain (CC0 1.0):
copy it, change it, use it for anything. Kestrel Bay is an invented place,
so a correct answer can only come from this file, never from what a model
already knows.

## The instrument

The Kestrel Bay tide gauge measures the height of the sea every six minutes.
Its float does not sit in open water. It sits inside a stilling well: a
vertical pipe, closed at the bottom except for a narrow intake hole. Water
can only enter and leave the pipe through that small opening, so the
stilling well damps the waves. Short wind waves and boat wakes are averaged
out, while the slow rise and fall of the tide passes through.

## Keeping it honest

Twice a year the keepers check the gauge against a tide staff bolted to the
harbour wall and against a survey benchmark cut into the lighthouse steps.
Silt can narrow the intake hole until the well lags behind the sea, so the
intake is cleared at every check. The keepers record the lag they find
before clearing it, because a growing lag is the first sign of a blocked
well.
